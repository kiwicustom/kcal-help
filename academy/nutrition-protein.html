Protein

Protein is the nutrient that most reliably supports muscle, recovery, and fullness. Make it a daily habit, not a weekend hero meal.

⏱ 8 min Beginner
💬 Buddy Tip

Anchor each main meal with a clear protein source before you add the rest of the plate.

🎯 Today's Mission

Hit a protein-forward lunch today and log it — notice afternoon hunger afterward.

WHY?

Why this matters

Adequate protein helps preserve lean mass when you are in a calorie deficit and supports repair when you train. It also tends to increase satiety, which makes an energy budget easier to live with.

Many people “eat healthy” yet drift low on protein because breakfasts are toast-heavy, lunches are salad-only, and dinners carry most of the day’s protein too late to help hunger earlier.

Protein quality and distribution matter. Spreading protein across meals usually beats one giant evening hit for comfort and muscle protein synthesis signals — especially when leucine-rich sources are included.

WHAT?

What to understand

Practical protein literacy.

  1. 1

    Think in anchors: eggs, dairy/quark, fish, poultry, lean meat, tofu/tempeh, legumes + complementary foods

  2. 2

    Per-meal targets often beat “I’ll catch up at night”

  3. 3

    Plant patterns can work — plan combining and total amount carefully

  4. 4

    Protein foods still contain calories; they are not free

  5. 5

    Consistency across the week matters more than one perfect day

Myth
More protein is always better — I’ll eat 300g.

Truth: Extremes crowd out fibre and micronutrients and are hard to sustain. Aim for enough, distributed, and enjoyable.

Myth
If I lift, protein powder is mandatory.

Truth: Powder is a convenience tool. Food-first works; powder helps when life is busy.

HOW?

How to do this in real life

Design defaults, not willpower moments.

  1. 1

    Pick a default breakfast protein (eggs, skyr/quark, protein yoghurt, tofu scramble)

  2. 2

    Build lunches around a palm-sized protein portion you can repeat

  3. 3

    Shop a weekly protein list before snacks

  4. 4

    Log protein-containing foods carefully — Buddy’s estimates improve when you confirm portions

  5. 5

    Review Progress weekly for protein consistency, not only calories

How Buddy helps

Buddy and Foodiary surface protein alongside energy so you can coach the plate, not only the total. Progress trends show whether anchors are sticking.

Hunger and adherence

If you are “good on calories” but ravenous, check protein and fibre before slashing further. Under-fuelled, low-protein days feel like failure of character when they are often failure of plate design.

Training days

Pair training with protein-containing meals in the hours around sessions when possible. You do not need perfection — you need repeatable patterns.

⚠ Common mistakes

Common mistakes

  • “Salad with a few shrimp was my high-protein lunch.”

    Garnish protein is not a portion. Weigh or estimate the actual protein food.

  • “I saved all protein for a huge dinner steak.”

    Distribution helps daytime hunger and training. Split across meals when you can.

  • “Plant protein doesn’t count.”

    It counts. Plan totals and complementary sources; don’t dismiss it or assume tiny portions suffice.

❤️ Encouragement

One protein anchor per meal is a quiet superpower. Repeat it until Buddy’s weekly view looks calmly green more often than not.
