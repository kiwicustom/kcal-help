Meal Prep

Meal prep is batching future decisions. A few containers of protein, plants, and smart carbs turn chaotic weekdays into automatic wins.

⏱ 9 min Beginner
💬 Buddy Tip

Prep components, not seventeen unique finished plates — mix-and-match keeps boredom away.

🎯 Today's Mission

Cook one protein batch and one fibre side you can reuse for two lunches; log today’s portion accurately including oil.

WHY?

Why this matters

Prep attacks the same problem as meal planning: empty-fridge evenings. It is especially powerful for lunch at work, where defaults otherwise become pastries and guesswork.

Prep fails when it is overly ambitious or when food quality dies by day three. Start small: one protein, one carb, two vegetables — 60–90 minutes once or twice a week.

Logging prepped food still matters. Homemade is not automatically tracked. Weigh a representative portion once so Buddy’s estimates match your containers.

WHAT?

What to understand

Prep that survives contact with real weeks.

  1. 1

    Component prep > elaborate multi-day tasting menus

  2. 2

    Cool food safely; refrigerate promptly; freeze extras

  3. 3

    Sauces separate to avoid sogginess

  4. 4

    Label containers with rough grams if that helps you

  5. 5

    Leave one flexible dinner un-prepped for freshness and social life

Myth
Meal prep requires a perfect Sunday and identical meals daily.

Truth: Short midweek refresh cooks also count. Variety can come from spices and sides.

Myth
If I meal prep, I don’t need to log.

Truth: You still log portions. Prep makes portions easier — it doesn’t replace Foodiary.

HOW?

How to do this in real life

Run a minimal viable prep session.

  1. 1

    Pick protein (chicken, mince, tofu, eggs, lentils) and cook a batch

  2. 2

    Roast or steam two vegetables; cook a carb staple

  3. 3

    Portion into containers sized for your real hunger — not aspirational tiny boxes

  4. 4

    Measure oil used in cooking and divide across portions when logging

  5. 5

    Schedule a 20-minute midweek top-up if needed

  6. 6

    On eating days: plate, log, go

How Buddy helps

Repeat logs get faster once a meal template exists. Meal Plan can mirror prep intent; Home captures when you eat the container.

Safety and quality

Use shallow containers, refrigerate within two hours, and follow sensible leftovers timelines. When unsure, freeze.

Reheat thoroughly. Texture issues? Keep crunchy add-ons (fresh veg, yoghurt) for day-of.

Family prep

Cook a shared base; customise kids’ or partners’ plates at the end. One kitchen session, many outcomes — Scale Health Success in a lunchbox.

⚠ Common mistakes

Common mistakes

  • “I prepped five identical dry chicken trays and quit by Wednesday.”

    Boredom is predictable. Components + sauces + rotation fix this.

  • “I guessed oil as zero because it was meal prep.”

    Batch oil still lands in each box. Divide it.

  • “I spent five hours prepping and burned out.”

    Overbuild kills the habit. Shrink the session until it feels easy.

❤️ Encouragement

One batch of protein in the fridge is a kindness to midweek-you. Start there — excellence is optional; repeatability is not.
