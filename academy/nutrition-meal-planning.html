Meal Planning

Meal planning reduces decision fatigue. You decide once when you have calm — then execute on busy days when willpower is tired.

⏱ 9 min Beginner
💬 Buddy Tip

Plan fewer meals than you think. Two reliable dinners and a default lunch beat a colourful schedule you abandon.

🎯 Today's Mission

Write tomorrow’s lunch and dinner before bed — realistic foods already in the house or on tomorrow’s shop path.

WHY?

Why this matters

Unplanned evenings default to the easiest delivery app. That is not a character flaw; it is empty fridge plus fatigue. Planning is environmental design.

Plans are intent — not the live diary. You still log what you actually eat on Home. If the plan changes, the log tells the truth; the plan simply made a good default available.

Planning also supports protein and fibre consistency. When anchors are pre-decided, you stop hoping lunch “somehow” becomes high-protein.

WHAT?

What to understand

What a good plan includes — and excludes.

  1. 1

    A short list of dinners you can cook in your real time budget

  2. 2

    A default breakfast and lunch pattern for weekdays

  3. 3

    Protein and fibre called out explicitly

  4. 4

    One flexible slot for restaurants or leftovers

  5. 5

    A shop list that matches the plan — not aspirational recipes you’ll never cook

Myth
If I deviate from the plan, I failed.

Truth: Plans guide. Logs record. Deviation is data — update next week’s plan.

Myth
Meal Plan replaces Foodiary.

Truth: Meal Plan is forward-looking. Foodiary/Home capture what happened. Both matter.

HOW?

How to do this in real life

Run a weekly 15-minute planning ritual.

  1. 1

    Check calendar: late nights, training, travel, social meals

  2. 2

    Pick 2–4 dinners; repeat favourites on purpose

  3. 3

    Assign protein anchors to breakfast/lunch defaults

  4. 4

    Write the shop list from the plan only

  5. 5

    Enter intent in Meal Plan if you use it; log reality on Home when you eat

  6. 6

    Sunday review: which planned meals actually happened?

How Buddy helps

Meal Plan holds intent and can support shopping helpers. Home logging keeps Progress honest when life edits the plan.

Templates over novelty

Rotation beats novelty for adherence. Keep a personal “greatest hits” list of 8–10 meals.

Introduce at most one new recipe per week while habits are forming.

Family and Spaces

If you cook for others, plan shared bases (protein + carb + veg) with optional sauces so one kitchen serves many preferences.

Scale Health Success starts at the dinner table — planning makes healthy the path of least resistance for more than one person.

⚠ Common mistakes

Common mistakes

  • “I planned seven gourmet dinners and ordered pizza on Tuesday.”

    Overambitious plans collapse. Match cooking skill and time.

  • “I planned but never shopped.”

    A plan without a list is a wish. Shop the same day you plan when possible.

  • “I logged the plan instead of what I ate.”

    That corrupts Progress. Log reality; keep the plan as a guide.

❤️ Encouragement

A humble plan you follow most days will outperform a perfect menu you admire. Decide tonight’s default — future-you will thank you.
